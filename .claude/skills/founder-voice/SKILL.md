# Founder Voice Profile

> **This file is a scaffold, not content.** Every section below is a prompt. Answer them in the
> founder's own words - ideally lifted from things they have already said out loud - and delete the
> instructions as you go. A voice profile written *about* someone in generic marketing language will
> produce generic marketing copy; one built from their actual sentences will not.

## Who the founder is

### The short version
Two or three sentences. What they do, who they do it for, and the thing they are unwilling to
compromise on. If it could appear on a competitor's About page, it is not specific enough yet.

### The longer version
The route that got them here, told as history rather than positioning. What they were doing before,
what changed their mind, what they got wrong once and now overcorrect for. Concrete beats flattering.

## Core convictions

Three to six. Each one gets a name, a one-line statement, and the reason it is held - usually a
specific experience rather than an argument. These are what let the agent take a position instead of
hedging, so vague convictions are worse than none.

Format each as:

**1. [Short memorable name]**
The claim in one sentence. Then two or three sentences on where it came from and what it rules out.

## Emotional register

### The founder sounds like
Five to ten adjectives or short phrases, each with a clause of evidence. "Direct - answers the
question asked, then stops."

### The founder does NOT sound like
The failure modes, stated as bluntly as you can stand. This section does more work than the one
above it, because most bad drafts fail by drifting into a register the founder would never use.

### The posture
Peer, teacher, contrarian, practitioner, host? Say which, and say what the reader is assumed to
already know. Posture decides whether a sentence reads as generous or condescending.

## Communication style

Fill in the patterns that actually recur. Common ones worth capturing:

- **Analogies** - are they primary or occasional? Drawn from what domains?
- **Stories over statements** - does a claim usually arrive attached to a specific incident?
- **Closing, not hedging** - does the founder end on a position or on a question?
- **Sentence rhythm** - long-then-short? fragments allowed? em-dashes or never?
- **Humour** - present, dry, absent? Self-deprecating or observational?

## Hard constraints

Words and constructions the founder does not use, with no exceptions. Be specific and be petty about
it - this is the part that most visibly separates their writing from a language model's. Include
punctuation preferences (straight vs smart quotes, en vs em dashes) and any opener patterns that are
banned outright.

## The Voice Test

Six questions to run against a finished draft before it goes to the founder. Write yours; these are
the shape:

1. Would the founder say this sentence out loud, to a peer, unprompted?
2. Is there a specific incident in here, or only claims?
3. Does it take a position that someone could disagree with?
4. Has anything been added to sound impressive rather than to be understood?
5. Is the opening line something a person would actually say first?
6. If you removed the byline, would a reader who knows the founder still recognise it?

A draft that fails any of these goes back, not forward.

## Keeping this current

Voice drifts, and a stale profile quietly stops matching. When the founder edits a draft, ask *why*
they made the change and write the answer into `style-observations` rather than patching this file
for every small correction. Revisit this profile when the observation log starts contradicting it.
