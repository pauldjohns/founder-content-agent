# Audience Profile — [role you write for]

> Template. Fill each section from research and real conversations, not from imagination. The
> reviewer skill scores drafts against this file, so a vague profile produces vague scoring.

## Role and seniority
Job titles, level, team size, who they report to, what they own and what they only influence.

## Pain points and motivations
What is hard in their week, in their words where you have them. Separate the pain they complain
about publicly from the one they only admit privately - the second is usually the real one.

## Content triggers
What makes this person stop scrolling: a number that contradicts their assumption, a named tool, a
situation they were in last week, a position their boss disagrees with. List the specific triggers,
not "useful insights".

## Language and tone
The words they use for their own work. The words that mark an outsider. How much jargon reads as
fluent versus as trying too hard. Whether they respond to certainty or to hedged honesty.

## Trusted voices and sources
Who they already read. Useful both for calibration and for knowing which arguments they have heard
a hundred times.

## Disqualifiers
Roles and segments that look adjacent but are not this audience. Write these down - they are what
stops a draft drifting toward whoever is easiest to write for.
